Loading paths

Proportional stress paths σ = λ·d starting from the current state; set the initial compacity with the model's compacity slider above. The path is driven by density: the compacity is stepped from its initial value up to full density (ρ = 0.999), and for each state the stress that keeps σ on the hardened surface (f = 0) is found, with associated flow giving plastic compaction εvp = −tr εp = ln(ρ/ρ₀). When the flow at first yield is dilatant (tension, shear, or the dry side of the cap), the compacity is stepped down instead, toward the loosest state (tap density, or 1 − 1/q₁ for Gurson): the material loosens or its voids grow, and the stress softens, stays constant on a shear line, or ends at a critical state where the flow turns isochoric. A compacting path stops early (×) when its flow turns isochoric or dilatant. Full density needs a very large stress, so the stress axis is logarithmic; in 3D the rays are cut at the edge of the view. Elastic strains are left out. Click a path name to select it for the run; click its swatch to hide it.

Reading the plots

Conventions. Tension positive. I₁ = tr σ, J₂ = ½ s:s, J₃ = det s. Haigh–Westergaard coordinates ξ = I₁/√3, ρ = √(2J₂); Lode angle θ with sin 3θ = −(3√3/2)·J₃/J₂3/2, so θ = −30° on the tensile meridian and +30° on the compressive one.

π-plane. Section of the surface at the current ξ (slider or sweep). T and C mark the tensile and compressive meridians; the dashed circle passes through the compressive meridian, so any Lode-angle (J₃) dependence shows as a gap. An overlay is drawn dashed in the second colour.

Plane stress. Intersection with σ₃ = 0 in σ₁–σ₂, also drawn dashed in 3D. Uniaxial strengths are read along the σ₁ axis. For cap models the loading paths are drawn as rays.

Meridians. Upper curve: tensile meridian (θ = −30°). Lower curve, mirrored: compressive meridian (θ = +30°). Compression is to the left.

Compaction & densification. Plastic compaction εvp and compacity ρr against the applied stress (log scale) for each loading path; × marks where a path stops.
