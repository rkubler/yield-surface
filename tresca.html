Tresca criterion: the polynomial form

Tresca's criterion is usually written with the maximum shear stress, which has corners. Lubliner (1990) gives it as one smooth polynomial in J₂ and J₃. That polynomial does not describe only the hexagonal prism: its zero set is six full planes, which bound two triangular prisms rotated by 60°. The Tresca prism is the part they share.

Formulations

CLASSICAL FORM · IN PRINCIPAL STRESSES

POLYNOMIAL FORM IN J₂ AND J₃ (LUBLINER, 1990)

Lubliner writes it with the shear yield stress k = σ₀/2: 4J₂³ − 27J₃² − 36k²J₂² + 96k⁴J₂ − 64k⁶ = 0.

FACTORED IN PRINCIPAL STRESSES (EXACT IDENTITY)

LODE-ANGLE FORM

IN PLANE STRESS, σ₃ = 0

KEEPING ONLY TRESCA

Elastic domain: F < 0 and 3J₂ < σ₀², which is the same as max|σᵢ − σⱼ| < σ₀.

Reading the picture

  1. Each factor (σᵢ − σⱼ)² − σ₀² vanishes on two parallel planes, σᵢ − σⱼ = +σ₀ and σᵢ − σⱼ = −σ₀. Three factors give six planes, all parallel to the hydrostatic axis, so every section along that axis is the same.
  2. The three “+σ₀” planes cannot meet at one point, because (σ₁ − σ₂) + (σ₂ − σ₃) + (σ₃ − σ₁) = 0 ≠ 3σ₀. They close into a triangular prism, A. The three “−σ₀” planes form the mirror prism B, rotated by 60°.
  3. In the π-plane the two triangles draw a six-pointed star (a hexagram). The region inside both is where all three |σᵢ − σⱼ| ≤ σ₀, which is exactly the Tresca hexagon.
  4. At the six corners two planes cross, so two factors vanish together. F has a double root there and its gradient is zero: the flow direction is not defined, as expected for Tresca. Along the equibiaxial path (σ, σ, 0), F = −σ₀²(σ² − σ₀²)², which touches zero at σ = σ₀ without changing sign.
  5. F < 0 inside the hexagon, but also in six half-strips that start at its corners and run outward. There one |σᵢ − σⱼ| is below σ₀ and the other two are above it, so one factor is negative and F is negative again. The sign of F alone therefore does not define the elastic domain.
  6. The missing condition is 3J₂ ≤ σ₀², the inside of the von Mises cylinder through the six corners. The extra parts of the planes and the six negative half-strips all lie outside it, and between the hexagon and the circle F > 0. A finite-element code using the polynomial can apply this check, or use the Lode-angle form directly.
